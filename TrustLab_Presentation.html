Act I  ·  The choice

Every act of trust is a bet on someone else's next move

Negotiation

Do I reveal my real limit?

Business

Do I deliver before they pay?

Relationships

Do I forgive the last time?

Strategy

Do I hold back or strike first?

History, incentives and uncertainty shape the answer. Can a model learn that from behaviour alone?

Our research question

Act IV  ·  The prediction

Adding strategy clues made the model better at telling cooperators from defectors

0.845 → 0.898

cross-validated ROC-AUC, averaged over 12 experimental sessions

In plain words  Shown one cooperating moment and one defecting moment, the model ranks them correctly about 90 times in 100, up from about 85.

Test accuracy 83.8% → 85.7%. The first model only matched the rule "repeat your last move" (83.8%).

Act V  ·  What we found

Three things the data showed us

Familiarity
56% vs 29%

Cooperation was about twice as common with a fixed partner as with changing partners.

Patterns
4 strategies

Players fell into four recognisable ways of playing, and the mix shifts with the setting.

Prediction
0.845 → 0.898

Adding strategy information improved next-move prediction (cross-validated ROC-AUC).

188 players, one payoff matrix, one-round memory: patterns in this experiment, not laws of human nature.
